01The idea

A second wind is the turn that arrives after the point you thought you were finished. Breath returns, momentum comes back, you finish stronger.

That's the whole name, and it's a better story than any tagline. So the idea lives in the shape and movement of the identity, never in motivational copy. And it has to sit in HUB's room the way the walnut and bronze do: warm, refined, made to last.

So the mark has to do three things. Say something about the name. Feel like a club, not a chain. And survive a one-metre sign, an embroidered cap and a 16-pixel favicon without changing character.

04Lockups

Two forms. The full mark wherever there's width, and the icon alone wherever the name is already known: on equipment, caps, the app, the glass, the floor.

Clear space is a quarter of the cap height on all sides; in practice, more. Minimum sizes are where the counters and fine parts start to close in print and thread.

05Colour

The mark is one colour. The palette comes straight off HUB's finish board, so the brand and the room are the same thing: linen as the field, walnut as the brand colour, charcoal for type and the mark, bronze in small amounts.

Walnut is the timber joinery and the café counter. Bronze is the tapware, the trims and the metal. Concrete is the floors and the light stone. Charcoal is the equipment frames and the gym walls. Saddle, the tan of the booths and upholstery, is used sparingly. Warm light, warm palette: nothing cool, nothing neon.

HUB's second revision adds olive: the turf and the café chairs. It belongs to the room, not the brand, so it never carries type or the mark. It just has to sit happily next to everything else, and it does.

06Typography

The mark is not a font, so the fonts have to sit beside it without competing. Barlow Condensed for display, its semi-condensed sibling for everything read, DM Mono for labels and numbers, Cormorant italic for the editorial voice.

All four are open licence, which keeps a ten-year lease simple. If Sergio wants more character in display later, Druk or Roobert are the candidates, flagged for cost.

07In the space

HUB's second revision is the reference. You arrive through the building's own foyer, from St Georges Terrace or the lifts; the café is a glass box on that foyer, and the gym is behind it. There is no separate gym door: the café is the front door, for members and for the office next door alike.

The full name appears in three places only: over the café doors, on the café counter wall and on the gym's end wall. Everywhere else the symbol does the work alone, on the reeded glass beside the doors, on the glass between café and gym, at the start of the turf track, and on equipment. The icon reads at a glance from across the floor, where a full name wouldn't.

The equipment is Sergio's own selection: Panatta, Nautilus and Citadel Strength, one piece at a time. All three make to order, so the frames and pads are specified in one colour across the floor, black frames and saddle pads, and the manufacturers' badges are the only other marks on the floor. 08The café

The café is the front door, open to everyone on St Georges Terrace. It's the most-seen part of the brand: far more people will hold a Second Wind cup than will ever walk onto the gym floor.

So there's no separate café logo. Same mark, same palette, same type. The symbol goes on everything a hand holds; the full lockup goes on the shopfront and the counter wall. The café leans lighter and warmer than the gym: linen, walnut and concrete, with HUB's olive in the chairs.

Slogans follow one rule: three or four words, stacked, Barlow Condensed capitals, a full stop, a short rule underneath. HUB's "Fuel good days" and "Stronger every day" already fit it.

09Social

The symbol is the avatar. It's the one place the mark is seen at thumbnail size a thousand times a day, and the icon has to survive that where a wordmark won't.

The Facebook cover is the primary lockup on linen with one line of fact, no photo, because a cover photo gets cropped differently on every device and the mark can't be at the mercy of that. The Instagram grid alternates photo, type and build-progress tiles so the feed reads as one brand before anyone opens a post.

11Digital

The website should feel like walking into HUB's room: warm, open and lit, not the typographic-logo-plus-curated-photos template every gym uses. Linen field, walnut anchors, big display type, the actual floor in the photos, the symbol as the only ornament.

The app icon is the symbol on walnut. Notifications are written in the voice: a coach's message, not a system's.

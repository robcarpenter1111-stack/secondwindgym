My recommendation

Sergio, you've seen everything you asked for on the other page. This is what I'd do if it were my call: one system for the club and the café, built from your notes, with a few refinements of my own that I've explained below. Nothing here replaces what you asked to see; it's my advice alongside it.

Why this works

It keeps everything you liked: the two strokes, bold and leaning for movement, a little more extended for presence, the cut tying the letters to the icon, and the serif giving the café its own calm voice.

Two rules that keep it premium

The serif is for the café's name only; menus, prices and signage text stay in the brand's sans. And the lean lives in the logo only; headings and the website stay upright. That discipline is what makes it feel expensive rather than busy.

The café name

First Wind Café is in as your working name. The one-stroke idea works with any name, so we can settle it together; before it goes on a cup or a sign we'll check nobody nearby already trades under it.

A refinement I'd make: on 01 and the variations you asked for, ATHLETIC CLUB was stretched to the full width of the name, which spaced the letters so far apart they were hard to read and too thin to light. It now leans with the letters and finishes flush under WIND, so the strokes open the mark and the club line closes it. It's a small change, but it's the difference between a logo and a well-drawn logo.
